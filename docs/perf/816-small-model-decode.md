### Gap trace, one locked window (round 3)

Phi3-mini decode b1, nsys timeline of 62 steps (CUDA graph replay, `cuda,osrt`): the graph is already in use (62 `cuGraphLaunch`, 7.9 ms total host time). Span of all kernels 2222 ms against kernel sum 2208 ms, so the GPU is 99.4% busy. Gaps over 50 us: 65 of them, 14.0 ms in total (the largest 3.2 ms, the first step), plus 1.0 ms of small gaps. Per step: 1 DtoH of the sampled token, 4 small HtoD copies, no other sync. The earlier "12% gap" was an artefact of comparing a wall figure from a shared GPU against a profile taken at another time: the in-process step time ranged 38.7 to 41.6 ms across rounds while the kernel sum is 35.9 ms per step, and the rest is GPU contention from other jobs on the same card (the DtoH wait absorbs it, 2.2 s over 62 calls). There is no host-side cause to fix.

qwen3-0.6b: step 7.24 to 7.31 ms in process against 7.10 ms kernel sum per step, a 2% gap, so the kernel sum is close to wall time. The gap is not the lever; the kernels are. The tiny-tile variants measured above give no 5% gain, so no split-k kernel was built: down and o together are 25% of the step, and the 6 stage variant, the only one that moved their medians (about 12%), moved the step by under 1%.

The scoreboard rows for qwen3 and phi3 were regenerated by `perfpass.sh` under the lock (qwen3 145.9 tok/s, phi3 25.3 tok/s).

### Round 4: bf16 batch-1 GEMV (tried, not kept)

A warp-per-2-rows bf16 GEMV (16 byte streaming loads, x in shared memory, warp-shuffle reduce, residual, bias and gate/up activation epilogues) was selected for tokens == 1 on qkv, o, gate_up, down and lm_head. Three alternating rounds under the lock, decode b1 tok/s (base / gemv):

| model | round 1 | round 2 | round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3-0.6b | 134.4 / 131.3 | 130.1 / 131.5 | 132.0 / 129.6 |
| phi3-mini | 24.3 / 23.8 | 24.0 / 24.1 | 23.4 / 23.9 |

Per kernel, nsys median us (tiny mma / gemv): gate_up 57.0 / 57.8, qkv 38.6 / 39.1, down 34.8 / 31.1, o 23.3 / 20.9, lm_head 1343 / 1404. The GEMV is faster on the two smallest linears (down 11%, o 10%) and equal or slightly slower on the rest, so the end to end step does not move (inside the 5% gate, noise about 3%). The tiny mma tile was not wasting bandwidth: lm_head at 232 GB/s and gate_up at 221 GB/s are what this GB10 delivers for a streaming read, and a dedicated GEMV reads the same bytes at the same rate. The 88% of 273 GB/s target is above that measured streaming ceiling, so it cannot be reached by a different GEMV. The GEMV was removed again: it also failed `specrt --test codegen` (`every_dispatched_kernel_is_generated` needs smem entries for new kernels, and `gemm_and_ggml_builders_use_prims_unless_listed` rejects a verbatim kernel text file), and keeping a path with no measured gain would only add maintenance.
